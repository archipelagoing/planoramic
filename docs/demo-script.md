# Hearth demo script

About 90 seconds. Say the quoted parts; the bracketed lines are recording cues. Start with the TV app already paired and showing events.

## Introduce it

[Show the physical TV with Hearth open.]

“Hey, I’m Archi, and this is Hearth. Basically, I wanted a shared calendar on the TV so you could just look up and see what’s going on, instead of everyone having to open their phones.

“This is running on my actual Fire TV Stick HD, using Vega OS.”

## Show the calendar

[Show the Next 7 days view. Point out the calendar names underneath events.]

“It connects to Google Calendar and brings your personal and shared calendars into one view. You can see the event, when it’s happening, and which calendar it comes from.”

[Use the remote to select Today, then return to Next 7 days.]

“I can switch between today and the next seven days using the remote.”

## Open an event

[Move to an event, press Select, show its details, then return. Choose an event with details suitable for a public video.]

“And if I want more information, I can select an event and open the details without picking up another device.”

[Point out the Next up card.]

“This also keeps the next upcoming event visible.”

## Explain the connection

[Briefly show the signed-in connection page on your computer, or keep the TV in view while explaining. You do not need to unpair it.]

“To connect it, you sign in with Google on your computer and enter the pairing code from the TV. The backend handles Google access, so you don’t have to type your login with the remote.”

## Close

[Return to the TV calendar.]

“The browser version has more features, but I narrowed this TV version down to getting the actual calendar working on the device.

“Next I want to make pairing persist when the app closes, add calendar visibility controls, and then work on AI scheduling help.

“But this is Hearth v1: a working shared calendar on Fire TV.”

## Before recording

- Use events you are comfortable making public. Keep account details, credentials, and unrelated browser tabs out of the shot.
- Keep the backend running and the TV connected. Avoid closing or unpairing the app during recording.
- Do one practice run of Today → Next 7 days → event details → return.
- Keep the whole video under three minutes, including any introduction or end screen.
- If you show the browser's extra features, identify them as browser features. AI scheduling is future work.
